What Desktag puts on your Mac

Every desktop visible, named, and one click away.

Desktag lives quietly in your menu bar with no Dock icon, keeps a small frosted sticker across every Mission Control desktop, and stays out of your way until you reach for it.

The sticker Free + Pro layouts

A floating tag bar that tucks cleanly into the screen edge.

Give your desktops short names, emojis, 9 calibrated colours (or automatic colour by number) and 8 distinct marks — star, heart, flag, bolt, leaf, moon, bookmark and pin. Click any tag to switch, click the active tag to edit, or hover to inspect its open windows.

  • Three Free layouts: Horizontal Bar, Vertical Stack and minimal Current-Desktop Pill, with Small, Regular and Large sizes plus opacity control.
  • Edge hiding & stepping aside (Free): Dock a sticker against a screen edge and it tucks into thin coloured stripes; point at the stripes to reveal it. Floating away from edges, it fades and passes clicks through when you work right next to it. Both pause automatically while VoiceOver is on.
  • Spatial Canvas & No-Boundary Canvas (Pro): Drop desktops together on a 2D board to create named coloured groups (with Clean Up and Undo), or switch to No-Boundary Canvas to float each desktop bubble independently anywhere on screen with magnetic alignment guides and 7 one-click presets.
Agents at work Desktag Pro

Know the moment an agent finishes, fails, or waits for you.

When you run coding agents or long builds across three different desktops, checking back every minute breaks your flow. Desktag reads macOS’s own process counters for your terminals locally — no hooks, no shell plugins, and never reading terminal output.

  • Live running light: A soft light animates under any desktop running an active coding agent or long command.
  • Clear alerts out of sight: When work stops, fails or asks for approval on another desktop, Desktag marks it with a colour-coded badge, menu bar dot, edge-docked done banner (dismissible without switching) or interactive macOS notification.
  • Go to Waiting Work shortcut: Bind one global key to jump directly to the next waiting desktop and bring its terminal window to the front, ringed.

Speed & window flow Free · Deep search in Pro

Drop windows onto any desktop. Jump from the keyboard.

Stop dragging windows against the edge of the screen waiting for Mission Control to crawl sideways. Desktag moves windows in one motion and turns switching into two keystrokes.

  • Drag onto any tag (Free): Release a dragged window on a tag to send it there while you stay (needs Accessibility), or hold a moment to switch along (no permission needed). Also works from Hover Cards, menus and the switcher (⌘↩), with Undo on every move.
  • Keyboard switcher (⌃⌥D): Type “ma” and press Return to jump to Mail. Press → to drill into a desktop’s open windows, ⌘1–⌘9 to pick a row, ⌘N for a quick note, or press Return on an empty query to toggle Back.
  • Direct-to-window deep search (Pro): Match open apps, terminal folders, window titles, agents and notes inside ⌃⌥D — pressing Return on a matched window takes you straight to it.
  • Hover cards everywhere (Free; folders & agents on Pro): Rest on any tag to inspect its note and windows, click an app to jump and ring its window, drag it onto another tag, or right-click to pull windows here.
Context & automation Spotlight & Services free · Automation in Pro

Unnamed desktops name themselves, and fit your macOS workflow.

Leave a desktop unnamed and Free labels it by open apps; Pro follows your live terminal and editor folders, Finder folders and page titles, disambiguating duplicate folder names automatically. Your own names always win.

  • Arrival Card & notes: Leave your next step on a desktop (1 note free, every desktop in Pro). When you arrive, a colour-tinted card shows the desktop’s name, note and age — hover to hold while reading.
  • Spotlight & macOS Services (Free & Pro): Search user-named desktops straight from Spotlight (⌘Space), or select text in any app and use Services › Desktag to name the desktop or save to its note.
  • Shortcuts, Focus Filters, links & AppleScript (Pro): 13 Shortcuts actions, macOS Focus Filters (auto-toggle Private Mode, hide the sticker or silence banners per Focus), desktag:// links, and built-in AppleScript / JXA scripting (Desktag.sdef).
  • Global shortcuts & menu bar scroll: Scroll the menu bar item to step across desktops, bind Back and Previous/Next Desktop hotkeys (Free), and assign 1 shortcut free or one-click ⌃⌥1–⌃⌥9 on Pro.

Folder naming reads your own terminal working directories locally; adding optional Screen Recording brings Finder folders, editor projects and page titles (in memory only, never saved).

With optional Screen Recording allowed, Desktag captures an ephemeral in-memory frame via ScreenCaptureKit so switches blend directly from the desktop you leave to the one you land on — skipping every desktop between, with nothing ever saved to disk. Respects macOS Reduce Motion.

Public APIs only

How it works, and where it stops.

macOS gives apps no public API to list Mission Control desktops, and the Mac App Store does not allow private ones. Desktag uses public APIs only: it places a tiny transparent 1×1-point marker window on each desktop it meets, asks macOS which marker is on the active space, and switches by bringing a marker forward. Everything below follows directly from that.

Meets desktops as you visit — or via Find Desktops

A desktop appears in the sticker the first time you visit it while Desktag is running, or all at once via Find Desktops (which walked 16 desktops in 5.5 seconds in testing).

After a restart

If Desktag restarts within a login, window numbers still match so every desktop stays recognised. After your Mac restarts, Desktag recognises desktops by their windows, silently syncs when you click any desktop (with Accessibility), or walks them via Find Desktops.

Accessibility is optional and posts input only

Only Find Desktops, direct ~0.1s switching, dropping a window to send it while you stay, and clicking an exact window from a card need Accessibility. Desktag reads nothing with it — the App Sandbox blocks reading other apps. Without it, switching slides via macOS’s Desktop & Dock setting, and holding a window over a tag still carries it along.

Process counters locally; Screen Recording optional

Terminal folders and agent activity come from what macOS lets any app read about your own processes (zero permission). Optional Screen Recording adds window titles (Finder folders, pages, agent tasks) and two-desktop transition blending — strictly in memory, never saved. macOS Sequoia+ may show a standard monthly reminder for that permission.

Full-screen apps & Mission Control markers

Full-screen apps have spaces of their own: Desktag shows the app’s name while you’re there, but can’t name or switch directly to full-screen spaces. Mission Control counts Desktag’s tiny transparent markers on each desktop, though nothing visible appears.

Multiple displays, Chrome profiles, Cursor & tmux

Multiple displays are designed for and not yet tested on physical multi-display hardware (with “Displays have separate Spaces” on, switching slides and matching is by windows only). Chrome window titles don’t expose profiles; Cursor’s in-editor agent transcripts sit outside the sandbox (Cursor’s terminals work normally); tmux sessions show through the pane running tmux only. Privacy by architecture Data Not Collected

No account. No tracking. No network access.

Desktag doesn’t ask you to trust a privacy promise alone — macOS enforces it. The app runs in the App Sandbox with no network entitlement, so it cannot open a connection to any server. Purchases are handled by Apple through StoreKit.

Sole entitlement: App Sandbox

No network client, no network server, no files outside Desktag’s container, no third-party SDKs.

Private Mode & screen sharing

Panels ask macOS to stay out of screen sharing. Private Mode hides titles, folders, notes and agent tasks everywhere — and turns on automatically while mirrored.

Memory-only titles & frames

Terminal commands mask passwords, tokens and keys. Optional Screen Recording titles and transition frames are never written to disk.

One local file, 7-day backups

Names, notes and arrangements live in one desktops.json file inside your Mac’s sandbox container, with daily backups and Undo.
